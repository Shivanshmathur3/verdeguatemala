## Bottleneck Diagnosis

For each stage transition, analyse the drop-off rate:

- **> 70% loss** → Critical bottleneck — investigate and fix urgently before any further outreach
- **50–70% loss** → Significant optimization opportunity — prioritize this quarter
- **< 50% loss** → Normal, monitor and improve incrementally

### Common causes by stage — and fixes

**Top → MQL (Lead quality problem)**
- Cause: ICP too broad, outreach list not filtered, wrong decision-maker contacted
- Fix: Run `lead-scorer` agent on all contacts before outreach. Only send Day 0 emails to Tier 1 and Tier 2. Remove companies with no India import history.

**MQL → SQL (Speed and qualification problem)**
- Cause: Lead response time > 24 hrs, no qualifying call within 48 hrs, generic pitch that doesn't address their specific sourcing need
- Fix: Respond to any inbound reply within 4 hours. Book a discovery call on the same day. Use SPIN questions (below) to uncover real need before pitching price.

**SQL → Proposal (Discovery depth problem)**
- Cause: Shallow discovery — sending a generic price sheet without understanding their volumes, current supplier, pain points, or project timeline
- Fix: Run the 5-question discovery framework before sending any quote. Use `proposal-generator` to create a personalized document, not a standard rate card.

**Proposal → Closing (Objection and champion problem)**
- Cause: Objections not addressed, multiple decision-makers with no internal champion, competitor with cleaner/faster offer
- Fix: Map objections (below). Identify and equip a champion inside the buyer org. Follow up within 24 hrs of proposal send with a WhatsApp message (visual + personal). Offer sample box as risk reversal.

## Response SLAs — Critical for Conversion

| Trigger | Required Response Time | Why |
|---------|----------------------|-----|
| New inbound lead (reply, form, DM) | ≤ 1 hour | Speed-to-lead: responding within 1 hr = 7× more likely to qualify. Within 5 min = 9× |
| After Day 0 email sent | Follow-up call within 48 hrs | Opens drop 70% after 48 hrs |
| After proposal sent | WhatsApp/email follow-up within 24 hrs | Buyers forget within 48 hrs |
| After proposal: no response | Day 3 → Day 7 → Day 14 follow-ups | Reduces ghost rate by 40% |
| Inactive lead (no movement 7 days) | Re-engage with new angle | Stale leads go cold permanently after 21 days |
| Re-engagement cadence | 30 days → 60 days → 90 days | Different message each time — never the same pitch |

## Qualification Frameworks

### BANT (simple cycles — first call with new importer)
Use for initial qualification of any new stone or jewellery importer lead.

| Letter | Question to ask |
|--------|----------------|
| **B**udget | "What's your typical annual stone / jewellery sourcing budget?" or "How many containers do you import per year?" |
| **A**uthority | "Who makes the final call on supplier decisions — is that you or someone else on your team?" |
| **N**eed | "What's driving your interest in sourcing from India right now? Is there a gap in your current supply?" |
| **T**imeline | "Are you looking to place an order this quarter, or exploring for next season?" |

---

### MEDDIC (complex B2B — project suppliers, hospitality chains, large distributors)
Use when selling to procurement teams, hotel groups, or large-scale construction project buyers.

| Letter | Meaning | Question |
|--------|---------|----------|
| **M**etrics | Quantify the economic impact | "If you had consistent Verde Guatemala supply at 15% below your current cost, what would that mean for your margin per project?" |
| **E**conomic Buyer | Find who controls the budget | "Who in your organization approves new supplier relationships?" |
| **D**ecision Criteria | What they evaluate suppliers on | "What are the top 3 things you need to see from a new stone supplier before you'll place a first order?" |
| **D**ecision Process | How they decide | "Walk me through how you'd typically evaluate and onboard a new supplier — what does that process look like?" |
| **I**dentify Pain | Surface the real problem | "What's your biggest frustration with your current India suppliers?" |
| **C**hampion | Find your internal advocate | "Is there someone on your team who's particularly keen on diversifying your supplier base? I'd love to get them on a call too." |

---

### SPIN Selling (discovery calls — to uncover real need before pitching)

| Letter | Type | Sample question |
|--------|------|----------------|
| **S**ituation | Fact-finding | "How are you currently sourcing green marble — direct from quarry or through a broker?" |
| **P**roblem | Surface pain | "What issues have you run into with your current suppliers — quality consistency, lead times, communication?" |
| **I**mplication | Amplify pain | "If those quality inconsistencies continue, what impact does that have on your projects and client relationships?" |
| **N**eed-Payoff | Get them to state the value | "If you had a direct quarry supplier in Udaipur with guaranteed consistency and 4-week lead time, how would that change things for you?" |

## Objection Handling — Top 5

| Objection | Root cause | Response |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| "We already have a supplier in India" | No perceived differentiation | "That's great — most of our best customers came to us as a second supplier for comparison. What variety do they mainly supply you? We might have something complementary." |
| "Your prices are too high" | Value not established before price | "Let's look at landed cost together — FOB is only part of the equation. Can I walk you through a total cost comparison including freight and quality loss rates?" |
| "We need to see samples first" | Trust deficit | "Absolutely — that's exactly what I'd want too. I'll send a complimentary DHL box of 6 varieties. Can I get your shipping address?" |
| "We're not sourcing right now" | Bad timing | "No problem at all. When does your next sourcing cycle typically start? I'll reach back at the right time with current lot photos." |
| "We've had bad experiences with Indian suppliers" | Previous trust damage | "I hear that a lot. May I ask what went wrong? We've been shipping containers to the US since 1999 — I'd like to show you specifically how we handle [their concern]." |
