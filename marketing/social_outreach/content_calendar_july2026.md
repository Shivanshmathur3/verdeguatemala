**Topic:** What exactly is Verde Guatemala? Most buyers get it wrong.

### LinkedIn Caption

"Verde Guatemala" is not a brand name. It is a geological classification — and most people sourcing it do not know what they are actually buying.

Here is what you need to know before your next purchase:

Verde Guatemala is a metamorphic rock — technically a serpentinite — quarried exclusively in the Guatemalan highlands. The green coloration comes from serpentine minerals formed under intense pressure over millions of years.

The five things that actually determine quality:

1. COLOUR CONSISTENCY — the green base should be uniform within a block. Wide variation between slabs from the same lot is a sign of mixed-block sourcing.

2. VEIN PATTERN — finer, more intricate veining commands a premium. Varieties like Spider Green and Imperial Green have distinctive patterns that increase perceived value.

3. SURFACE FINISH — Verde Guatemala polishes to an exceptionally high gloss. If the polish looks flat or cloudy, the slab was under-processed.

4. THICKNESS TOLERANCE — industry standard is +/- 1mm. Anything wider signals poor calibration and creates installation problems.

5. ORIGIN DOCUMENTATION — authentic Verde Guatemala comes with certificates of origin from Guatemala. If your supplier cannot provide this, you may be buying a substitute.

At Divya Stones, we have sourced Verde Guatemala exclusively since 1999. We know every quarry face, every block grade, and every exporter in the supply chain.

**Platform:** LinkedIn + Instagram

**Pillar:** P2 — Education
**Topic:** How to read a Verde Guatemala slab — what experienced buyers check that beginners miss

### LinkedIn Caption

When an experienced stone buyer looks at a Verde Guatemala slab for the first time, they check seven things in a specific order.

Most first-time buyers check one: colour.

Here is the full checklist — and why each point matters:

1. BACK OF SLAB FIRST: Flip the slab and look at the back surface before the polished face. The back shows you the natural mineral distribution without the distraction of the polish. Uneven colour on the back = colour inconsistency risk in the finished installation.

2. EDGE PROFILE: Run a fingernail along the cut edge. A clean, uniform edge means the block was cut at the right speed with a well-maintained blade. A chipped or ragged edge is a calibration warning.

3. HOLD TO DIRECT LIGHT AT A LOW ANGLE: This reveals micro-cracks, filler repairs, and surface inconsistency that you will never see head-on under flat lighting.

4. GLOSS CHECK WITH GLOSS METER: Polished marble should read 80–95 GU (Gloss Units) on a calibrated meter. Anything below 70 suggests under-polishing. Ask your supplier for the gloss standard on their polish line.

5. THICKNESS CONSISTENCY: Measure at four corners and the centre. More than 1.5mm variation across a slab is outside acceptable tolerance for precision installation.

6. WATER TEST: Wet the surface. Colour saturation and vein clarity should intensify evenly. Blotchy water absorption patterns indicate variable density — a structural inconsistency in the block.

7. MATCH TO REFERENCE SAMPLE: Hold your approved sample against the production slab under the same light source you used for approval. If the colour relationship shifts dramatically, something in the process changed.

Print this list. Use it every time.

**Topic:** The difference between "Verde Guatemala" and "Guatemala Green" — it matters legally

### LinkedIn Caption

Two names. Significant commercial difference.

"Verde Guatemala" is the internationally recognised geological and commercial classification for serpentinite marble quarried in the Guatemalan highlands. It appears on certificates of origin, customs documents, and official trade classifications.

"Guatemala Green" is a looser commercial term used — sometimes innocuously, sometimes deliberately — to describe stones that share a similar colour but may not originate from Guatemala at all.

Why does this matter for buyers?

1. CUSTOMS CLASSIFICATION: If your supplier's documentation uses "Guatemala Green" but your specification requires "Verde Guatemala," you may have a contractual gap that complicates claims or returns.

2. SUBSTITUTION RISK: Some exporters use "Guatemala Green" as a cover term for similar-looking stones from other origins — including synthetic or engineered products. The name gives them ambiguity. Your specification should eliminate it.

3. PRICE JUSTIFICATION: Authentic Verde Guatemala commands a price premium over generic green marble. If you are paying Verde Guatemala prices for a loosely classified product, you are overpaying for something that will not perform the same way.

4. RESALE AND DOCUMENTATION: For project documentation, heritage buildings, luxury residential resale, and export re-certification purposes, "Verde Guatemala" with a Guatemalan certificate of origin is the only classification that holds up to scrutiny.

Always ask for the certificate of origin from Guatemala. It is the only document that confirms what you are actually buying.
