# US Cold Call Kit — 10 Calls/Day Into Warmest Prospects
**Who gets called: opened the email twice, no reply (plan §2). Goal = PERMISSION to quote, not a close.**

## The 20-Second Opener (verbatim)

> "Hi [FIRST_NAME], this is [NAME] calling from Divya Stones in India — I'll be brief. We export the granite and marble you're already importing, and since the tariff was struck down in February, landed cost on Indian material is down about thirty percent. Can I email you a quote on what you're currently bringing in, so you can compare it against what you paid last year?"

**Then stop talking.** The ask is permission for ONE email. Almost nobody refuses a free comparison.

## Gatekeeper Variant (15 seconds)

> "Hi, this is [NAME] from Divya Stones — we're an Indian stone exporter. I'm trying to reach whoever handles your slab purchasing. It's about the tariff repeal and what it does to your landed costs — who's the right person?"

## Voicemail (20 seconds, always leave it)

> "Hi [FIRST_NAME], [NAME] from Divya Stones in India. Quick reason for the call: the tariff on Indian stone is gone and landed costs dropped about thirty percent — I wanted to send you a comparison quote on the material you're already importing. I'll follow up by email, or WhatsApp me anytime: nine-one, nine-four-one-four-one, six-seven-two-seven-eight. Thanks."

## Objection Responses (word for word, max 3 sentences)

**"We already restocked."**
> "That's fair — most bought early when the tariff dropped. When your next buying cycle comes around, wouldn't you want a second number to negotiate with? Let me send the quote; it costs you nothing to hold it."

**"Who are you? Never heard of you."**
> "Fair question — we've shipped from Udaipur since 2000, and you can verify every container on Panjiva or ImportGenius, search shipper 'Divya.' I'd rather you check us out than take my word. Can I email you our credentials with the quote?"

**"Just send me an email."**
> "Will do — one thing so it's actually useful: which two or three materials are you buying most right now? I'll quote those specifically instead of sending a generic price list."

**"We're happy with our current supplier."**
> "Good suppliers are worth keeping — I'm not asking you to switch. Most of our best accounts started as the comparison number. Can I be the quote you check theirs against?"

**"Your freight times are too long."**
> "Mundra to Houston runs about three to four weeks on water — with a three-week production lead, you're looking at six to seven weeks door to door. For planned restocking that's workable, and the landed savings usually pay for the planning. Want the math in the quote?"

**"We got burned by an Indian supplier before."**
> "I hear that more than I'd like, and I get it. That's exactly why we send numbered slab photos before crating and welcome third-party inspection — SGS, BV, or your own agent. May I ask what went wrong, so I can show you specifically how we handle it?"

**Order the daily block: East Coast first, West Coast last.**

## Daily 10-Call Block (70 minutes)
1. **Prep (10 min):** pull today's 10 from CRM (opened 2×, no reply), review each one's materials + port
2. **Calls (45 min):** dial all 10 — connect, voicemail, or gatekeeper; never skip the voicemail
3. **Log (15 min):** outcome per call + **the 2-hour rule: anyone who said "send it" gets the email within 2 hours, quote within 48**

## Post-Call Log Fields (CRM or sheet)
`date · company · contact · outcome (connected/voicemail/gatekeeper/wrong#) · objection heard · permission_given (Y/N) · materials_confirmed · follow_up_due`
