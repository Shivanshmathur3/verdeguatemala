## Script 1 — Cold Call Opener: Gatekeeper Version (30 seconds)

**Use when:** You reach a receptionist, assistant, or front-desk contact before reaching the decision maker.

**Goal:** Get the name and direct line of the person who handles stone purchasing — do not pitch to the gatekeeper.

---

> "Hi, this is Shivansh from Divya Stones in India — we're a Verde Guatemala marble quarry and direct exporter. I'm trying to reach the person who handles your stone or natural stone imports. Could you point me in the right direction, or let me know the best name to ask for?"

**If they ask what it's about:**
> "Of course — we supply Indian green marble directly from our quarry in Udaipur, and I wanted to introduce our pricing to the right buyer. It'll only take a couple of minutes of their time."

**If they say the person is unavailable:**
> "No problem at all. Could I get their name and email so I can send them a quick note? I'll keep it very brief."

**Notes:**
- Speak slowly and clearly — the gatekeeper may not be familiar with stone sourcing terminology
- Never say "I'm calling about a business opportunity" — it signals sales call and triggers screening
- If you get a name, use it in your follow-up email subject line immediately

## Script 2 — Cold Call Opener: Decision Maker Version (20 seconds)

**Use when:** You are connected directly to the buyer, purchasing manager, or owner.

**Goal:** Earn 2 minutes. Not to sell — to open a conversation.

---

> "Hi [Name], this is Shivansh from Divya Stones in Udaipur, India — we manufacture and export Verde Guatemala marble directly from our own quarry. I sent [Company] a pricing note earlier this week and wanted to make sure it landed with the right person. Do you have 2 minutes?"

**If they say "What's this about?":**
> "Of course — we supply Indian green marble direct to US importers, cutting out the broker layer. I wanted to see if the pricing and varieties we carry are relevant to what [Company] sources. Literally 2 minutes and I'll know if it's worth your time."

**If they say "Send me an email":**
> "Absolutely — I have it queued up. Just so I send the right things: are you currently sourcing any Indian green marble or Verde Guatemala? That way I won't waste your inbox."
*(This turns a brush-off into a qualifying question.)*

**If they say "Not interested":**
> "Completely understand — no pressure at all. If sourcing needs ever change, I'm Shivansh at Divya Stones. Have a good one."

**Notes:**
- The reference to the prior email gives credibility and context even if they never saw it
- Do not use filler phrases ("I was just calling to...") — they signal low confidence
- If they engage, move immediately into the discovery opener: "Before I tell you anything about us — how are you currently sourcing green marble?"

## Script 3 — Voicemail Script (20 seconds max)

**Use when:** The decision maker does not pick up. Leave this once only — do not leave multiple voicemails.

**Goal:** Create curiosity + give them two ways to respond (call back or WhatsApp).

---

> "Hi [Name], this is Shivansh from Divya Stones — Verde Guatemala marble exporter in Udaipur, India. I sent [Company] a pricing note this week and wanted to make sure it reached you. I'll also drop you a WhatsApp with a few lot photos — easiest to reach me at +91-94141-67278. Thanks, [Name], speak soon."

**Notes:**
- Stay under 20 seconds — long voicemails are deleted before they finish
- Mention WhatsApp explicitly — it sets up the next touchpoint and many stone buyers prefer it for photo sharing
- Do not say "I'll call again" — it sounds like a threat; let them choose how to respond
- Say your name and company clearly, twice if needed — international calls can be unclear

## Script 4 — WhatsApp Opener (Post-Email, Post-Call Attempt)

**Use when:** You have sent Email 1 and attempted a call (or left a voicemail). This is the third touchpoint.

**Goal:** Deliver visual proof (photos), establish a human channel, and invite a low-friction response.

---

> "Hi [Name], Shivansh here from Divya Stones in India. Sent you a note about Verde Guatemala marble pricing and also tried calling just now.
>
> Sharing a couple of photos from our current quarry lot so you have something visual to look at.
>
> [ATTACH: 2 photos — polished slab face + veining close-up]
>
> Happy to answer questions here or set up a quick call whenever suits you. What works best?"

**Variation — if they are a jewellery importer:**
> "Hi [Name], Shivansh here from Divya Stones in India. Sent you an email about our Jaipur jewellery range earlier this week.
>
> Dropping a few photos from our latest collection here — easier to see on WhatsApp than in an email.
>
> [ATTACH: 3 jewellery product photos]
>
> If any of these fit what you're sourcing, happy to send the full catalogue and pricing. What's the best way to connect?"

**Notes:**
- Keep the opening message under 5 lines — long walls of text on WhatsApp are ignored
- Send the photos as a separate message immediately after the text (do not embed them in the text bubble)
- The closing "What works best?" is intentionally open — it invites them to set the terms, which lowers resistance
- If there is no reply within 48 hours, one follow-up WhatsApp is acceptable: "Just checking this reached you, [Name] — happy to answer any questions."
- Do not send more than 2 WhatsApp messages without a reply

## Discovery Call Opener (bonus — for use once a call is booked)

**Use when:** You are on a scheduled call with a qualified prospect.

**Goal:** Understand their situation before pitching anything.

---

> "Thanks for making time. Before I tell you anything about Divya Stones, I'd love to get a better picture of your situation first — would that be okay?
>
> [pause for yes]
>
> How are you currently sourcing Indian green marble — or green stone more broadly? What's working well, and where are the friction points?"

**Follow-up discovery questions (use 2-3, not all):**
- "What does your typical order look like — volume, format, frequency?"
- "How important is price consistency vs. quality consistency for your buyers?"
- "Have you worked with Indian suppliers directly before, or mostly through US brokers?"
- "What would need to be true for you to switch or add a new supplier?"

**Notes:**
- Do not jump into your pitch until you have heard their answers to at least 2 questions
- Listen for the words "consistent," "reliable," "responsive" — these are the values your social proof emails are built around
- If they mention a pain point (e.g., "our current supplier keeps varying the colour"), write it down and reference it in your follow-up proposal
