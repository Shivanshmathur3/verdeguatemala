## How to Use This Document

These are ready-to-send reply templates. Each template has:
- A subject line (for email) — use exactly as written or adapt the bracketed fields
- The body copy — fill in all [BRACKETED] fields before sending; do not send with placeholders
- A "what not to do" note for the most common mistakes with each template
- The CRM action to take immediately after sending

Every message is written in plain, professional English. No jargon, no overstatement. The tone is confident and specific — Divya Stones knows its product and treats the buyer as a peer.

These templates are starting points. If you know something specific about the buyer — their city, a project they mentioned, a stone they carry — weave it in. Personalisation converts better than polish.

## Template 4 — Post-Call Follow-Up (Discovery Call)

**Response SLA:** 2 hours after call.

---

**Body:**

Hi [FIRST NAME],

Thank you for your time today — it was a genuinely useful conversation and I have a much clearer picture of what you're looking for.

Let me summarise what I understood from our discussion:

**Your requirement:**
- Product: Verde Guatemala [slabs / tiles / blocks] in [FINISH]
- Volume: approximately [X] containers per [year / season]

- Timeline: [WHAT THEY SAID]
- Key priorities: [e.g., colour consistency / batch documentation / payment terms / quick turnaround]

**What we discussed:**
- [KEY POINT 1 from the call — e.g., "You currently source green marble from X but have had issues with Y"]
- [KEY POINT 2 — e.g., "Your clients are primarily interior designers and retail showrooms in Z"]
- [KEY POINT 3 — e.g., "You need the first trial shipment to arrive before [date]"]

**Next steps agreed:**
- I will prepare a formal quotation for [FORMAT / VOLUME / INCOTERM] and send it to you by [DATE — within 7 days]
- [Any other next step you agreed on the call]
- Our next conversation is scheduled for [DATE / TIME] to review the proposal

If I've missed anything or misunderstood any detail, please correct me — I want to make sure the proposal I send is exactly aligned with what you need.

Looking forward to it.

Best regards,
[YOUR NAME]
Divya Stones — Verde Guatemala Export
Udaipur, Rajasthan, India
[PHONE / WHATSAPP]

---

**What Not To Do:**
- Do not skip the call summary section. Writing it out confirms you listened and forces you to surface any misunderstandings before the proposal stage.
- Do not leave the next step vague ("I'll be in touch"). Commit to a specific date for the proposal and a specific date for the next call.
- Do not send this the next morning. Momentum is highest in the 2 hours after the call.

**CRM Action After Sending:**
- Move lead to Discovery stage in CRM
- Log full call notes (not just summary — include anything useful: competitor names mentioned, budget signals, objections raised)
- Create task: "Send proposal" with due date set to the date you committed in the email
- Create task: "Proposal review call" with the agreed date and time

## Template 5 — Post-Proposal Follow-Up (24 Hours After Sending Quote)

**Trigger:** You sent a formal proposal or quotation yesterday. This is the first structured follow-up — it confirms receipt and opens the door to questions. It is not a chase; it is a service touchpoint.

**Response SLA:** 24 hours after proposal was sent.

---

**Subject:** Checking in — [COMPANY NAME] proposal | Divya Stones

**Body:**

Hi [FIRST NAME],

I wanted to follow up on the proposal I sent yesterday for [FORMAT — e.g., Verde Guatemala polished slabs, 20mm, CIF Rotterdam].

1. Did the document come through clearly? (Sometimes PDFs get clipped or filtered — happy to re-send in a different format if needed.)

2. Is there anything in the proposal you'd like me to walk through? Pricing structure, payment terms, lead time, or packing — I'm happy to jump on a 15-minute call if that's easier than going back and forth on email.

3. The pricing in the proposal is valid for 30 days from the date of issue — so [EXPIRY DATE]. I mention it only so you have full visibility on the timeline.

No pressure on speed — I want you to review it properly. But if there are any questions or if something doesn't fit your requirements, I'd rather know now so I can adjust the proposal than have you sit on something that doesn't quite work.

Looking forward to your thoughts.

Best regards,
[YOUR NAME]
Divya Stones — Verde Guatemala Export
Udaipur, Rajasthan, India
[PHONE / WHATSAPP]
[EMAIL]

---

**What Not To Do:**
- Do not make this sound like a chase. "Just checking in" as an opener feels low-value. Lead with a service offer (confirming receipt, offering to walk through it).
- Do not ask "what did you think?" — it puts the buyer on the spot. Ask specific, easy questions instead.
- Do not forget to include the proposal expiry date — it creates a soft urgency without pressure.

**CRM Action After Sending:**
- Log as "Proposal follow-up 1 sent"
- Set Day 5 reminder: if no response, send follow-up 2 (different angle — reference, alternative format, or a relevant market point)
- Set Day 10 reminder: if still no response, escalate — WhatsApp + email with revised offer or sample option

## Template 6 — Ghost Revival (30-Day Silent Lead Re-Engagement)

**Trigger:** A lead has been completely silent for 30 days despite prior follow-up attempts. They were previously engaged or interested at some level. This is the re-engagement touchpoint — one final, low-pressure attempt before moving them to Nurture or archiving.

**Response SLA:** This is a scheduled Nurture touchpoint — send on the scheduled day, not before.

---

**Subject:** Verde Guatemala — one thing that might be relevant for you

**Body:**

Hi [FIRST NAME],

I know we last spoke [X weeks / months] ago and I haven't heard back from you since — I don't want to keep chasing if the timing isn't right, so this will be a low-key message.

I'm reaching out because [PERSONALISED REASON — choose whichever is most honest and relevant]:

Option A (new product/availability):
We've just processed a new batch of Verde Guatemala slabs from our latest quarry extraction — the quality and veining in this lot are particularly strong, and I thought of you given what you mentioned about [SOMETHING FROM YOUR EARLIER CONVERSATION].

Option B (market development):
We've recently started working with a buyer in [THEIR REGION / A COMPARABLE MARKET] on a regular container program for Verde Guatemala. It made me think of your operation and whether your sourcing plans have changed since we last spoke.

Option C (simple check-in):
Our conversation a few months back suggested [YOU / YOUR MARKET] might be a good fit for Verde Guatemala at some point. I'm not sure if your sourcing plans have evolved, but I wanted to check in before I stop reaching out.

If the timing still isn't right, I completely understand — just let me know and I won't follow up again unless you reach out. No hard feelings at all.

And if something has shifted — a new project, a gap in your current range, a problem with your existing supplier — I'm happy to pick up the conversation wherever it makes sense.

Best regards,
[YOUR NAME]
Divya Stones — Verde Guatemala Export
Udaipur, Rajasthan, India
[PHONE / WHATSAPP]
[EMAIL]

---

**What Not To Do:**
- Do not reference how many times you have followed up ("I've reached out several times"). This creates guilt and resistance, not engagement.
- Do not make the email long. Ghost revival emails must be short. If they are not reading your emails, a long one will definitely not be read.
- Do not beg or over-apologise. The tone is warm and matter-of-fact, not desperate.
- Do not use a subject line that sounds like a chase (e.g., "Following up again" or "Did you see my last email?"). Lead with the value, not the follow-up.

**CRM Action After Sending:**
- Log as "Ghost revival sent — Day 30"
- If no response within 7 days: move to Nurture, set Day 60 and Day 90 touchpoints
- If they respond: move back to MQL immediately, create a follow-up call task within 24 hours
- If they ask to be removed from contact: mark as Closed Lost, loss reason "No response," do not contact again
