## Executive Summary

- The Middle East and Gulf region is India's single most strategically important natural stone export corridor. Saudi Arabia (688K tons) and the UAE (315K tons) collectively absorb roughly 50% of the Middle East's total marble building stone imports by volume, driven by Vision 2030 giga-projects (NEOM, Red Sea Project, Qiddiya) and Dubai/Abu Dhabi's sustained luxury construction pipeline.
- The GCC applies a standard 5% common external tariff on marble. Under the India-UAE CEPA (in force since May 2022), preferential rates apply to qualifying processed stone; the India-Oman CEPA signed in December 2025 opens additional duty-free access to Oman. Exporters must secure a Certificate of Origin (CO) to claim CEPA benefits.
- Verde Guatemala (Indian Green Marble) occupies a distinct niche: it has no direct competitor in the Gulf — Turkish travertine, Italian marble, and Chinese granite are not green marble substitutes. The key competitive threat is other Indian Verde Guatemala exporters from the same Udaipur/Rishabhdeo belt.
- Gulf buyers — particularly in Saudi Arabia and Qatar — strongly prefer polished slabs in large formats (60×60 cm, 60×120 cm, 80×160 cm); wall cladding grades for hotel lobbies and commercial towers are the highest-value segment. Consistency of veining across batch shipments is a non-negotiable requirement for project-grade procurement.
- Divya Stones' differentiation opportunity lies in quarry-direct provenance, consistent stone grading, willingness to supply cut-to-size/customised pieces, and presence at the Big 5 Marble & Stone World (Dubai, November 2026) and Stone & Surface Saudi Arabia (Riyadh, early 2026).

## 1. Market Size & Demand

### 1.1 Overall Market Scale

The Middle East natural stone market was valued at approximately USD 205M in 2024 in the processed/finished stone segment, projected to reach USD 363.5M by 2033 at a CAGR of 6.6% (Grand View Research). A broader valuation that includes raw blocks and quarry-gate value places the Middle East natural stone and marble market at ~USD 7B (2025), growing to USD 10B by 2031 at a CAGR of 9.4% (6W Research).

The Middle East marble building stone market specifically consumed approximately 2.03 million tons in 2024, recovering 15% year-on-year after two consecutive years of decline. The total value of this import segment was approximately USD 3.5B across the region.

## 5. Import Regulations & Duties

### 5.1 HS Code Classification

| HS Code | Description | Applicable to Divya Stones |
|---|---|---|
| **2515.11** | Marble and travertine — crude or roughly trimmed | Raw/rough blocks from quarry |
| **2515.12** | Marble and travertine — merely cut by sawing or otherwise, into blocks or slabs of rectangular shape | Gang-sawn slabs (unpolished) |
| **6802.21** | Marble, travertine, alabaster — merely cut or sawn, with a flat or even surface | Sawn tiles/slabs, not further worked |
| **6802.91** | Marble, travertine, alabaster — other (polished, shaped, profiled, etc.) | **Primary code for Divya Stones' polished slabs and tiles** |

### 5.2 UAE Import Duty

- **Standard GCC Common External Tariff:** 5% of CIF value — applies to all GCC member states (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, Bahrain) for most goods
- **VAT (UAE):** 5% VAT applied at import in UAE
- **Free Zone import:** Goods imported into Jebel Ali Free Zone (JAFZA) are exempt from customs duty; duty is paid only when goods exit the free zone into UAE mainland

### 5.3 India-UAE CEPA (Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement)

- **In force:** 1 May 2022
- **Scope:** UAE committed to eliminating tariffs on 97% of tariff lines (99% of Indian export value); 80.3% were immediately eliminated
- **Marble/Stone status:** The Finance Ministry revised CEPA tariff schedules via **Notification No. 08/2025-Customs (1 February 2025)** and subsequent **Notification No. 21/2025-Customs (April 2025)** and **Notification No. 18/2026-Customs**, introducing differentiated duty structures. New rates for marble/stone products under CEPA range from 8–20% *on the Indian side* (import into India); for Indian exports *to* UAE, the preferential rate reduces the UAE-side standard 5% — the exact rate for HS 6802.91 must be verified against the official CEPA tariff schedule on the Indian Trade Portal.
- **Important caveat:** India kept certain processed marble categories on its negative/sensitive list under CEPA. The UAE-side schedule must be checked separately. Exporters should verify the specific sub-heading rate at: https://indiantradeportal.in
- **To claim CEPA benefit:** Exporter must obtain a **Certificate of Origin (Preferential)** from FIEO, Export Inspection Council (EIC), or an authorised issuing body. This must be presented to UAE Customs at entry.

### 5.4 India-Oman CEPA

- **Signed:** December 2025
- **Terms:** Oman committed to duty-free access on more than 98% of tariff lines for Indian exports; phased elimination over up to 10 years for remaining lines
- **Stone-specific win:** Oman lifted its ban on exporting unpolished marble under this CEPA, providing Indian processors with a new raw material source. For Divya Stones exporting *to* Oman, preferential rates are available; verify HS 6802.91 rate on Indian Trade Portal.

### 5.5 Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait

- All apply the **GCC Common External Tariff of 5%** on CIF value for marble and natural stone
- No India-specific FTA is in force with Saudi Arabia, Qatar, or Kuwait as of June 2026 — standard 5% applies
- Saudi Arabia imposes **15% VAT** on goods sold domestically (not at import for B2B, but affects landed cost calculation for buyers)
- **Qatar:** No VAT currently; 5% customs duty only

### 5.6 Documentation Required

| Document | Issuing Body |
|---|---|
| Commercial Invoice | Exporter |
| Packing List | Exporter |
| Bill of Lading / Airway Bill | Shipping Line |
| Certificate of Origin (Non-Preferential) | Chamber of Commerce (Udaipur) |
| Certificate of Origin (Preferential, CEPA) | FIEO / EIC / authorised body |
| Phytosanitary Certificate | Not required for stone |
| Test/Quality Report (if required) | Accredited lab (e.g., NABL-certified) |
| CE Marking (for EU; not mandatory for Gulf) | Optional but adds credibility |
