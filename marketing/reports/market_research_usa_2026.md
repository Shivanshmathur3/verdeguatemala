### 4.1 HS/HTS Classification for Verde Guatemala Marble

| Product Type | HTS Code | Description |
|-------------|----------|-------------|
| Marble blocks (rough) | 2515.11.00 | Crude or roughly trimmed marble |
| Marble slabs (rough sawn) | 2515.12.10 | Simply cut, by sawing or otherwise |
| **Worked marble slabs / tiles (polished)** | **6802.91.xx** | **Marble, travertine, alabaster — worked; most common export format** |
| Marble tiles (≤ 9mm thick) | 6802.91.15 | Marble tiles, thin |
| Marble countertop slabs (>9mm) | 6802.91.20 | Marble slabs |
| Other worked calcareous stone | 6802.99.00 | If classification differs |

**Most of Divya Stones' export product (polished slabs 18–20mm) will classify under HTS 6802.91.** Always confirm the precise sub-heading with a licensed US customs broker before the first shipment.

### 4.2 US Tariff Rate Structure (June 2026)

The tariff regime on Indian natural stone has undergone dramatic change in the 2025–2026 period. Understanding this history is critical for buyer conversations.

| Period | Effective Tariff on HTS 6802 from India | Notes |
|--------|-----------------------------------------|-------|
| Pre-April 2025 | 0%–3.7% (base rate; GSP suspended since 2019) | Low-tariff era; strong India-US stone trade |
| April 2, 2025 | +26% reciprocal tariff announced (Liberation Day) | Trump administration |
| April 9, 2025 | 90-day pause; rate reverted to 10% baseline | Temporary relief |
| August 27, 2025 | 50% tariff imposed on Indian goods | Most severe — exports from India to USA "collapsed" |
| February 7, 2026 | US–India Interim Trade Framework: reciprocal tariff reduced to 18% | Current framework |
| **June 2026 (current)** | **~18% reciprocal + base HTS rate 4–6.5% = ~22–25% total** | **Under interim deal; BTA negotiations ongoing** |

**Current Effective Landed Cost Impact:**
- Pre-2025 baseline: Indian stone with 0–3.7% duty was extremely competitive.
- Under the current 18% reciprocal + base rate regime, the total tariff burden is approximately **22–25%**, adding roughly **USD 5–7 per sq metre** to the landed cost of polished slabs.
- This is significant but manageable — Turkish and Chinese stone faces **similar or higher reciprocal rates**, partially levelling the competitive field (China faces 145%+ tariffs on most goods as of 2026).
- The February 2026 US–India Interim Framework applies **18% on goods including home décor, artisanal products, and textiles**. Natural stone is categorised within the broader "building materials/home décor" grouping; however, the **full BTA has not been finalised**, and Chapter 68 stone products may receive further specific treatment. Buyers and exporters should monitor quarterly updates.

**Note:** India's GSP (Generalized System of Preferences) eligibility was terminated in June 2019 and has not been formally restored as of June 2026. Restoration discussions are ongoing as part of the BTA process.

| **Packaging failures** | Inadequate wooden crates lead to slab breakage on container floor; corner damage | Common | Use bubble wrap + foam corners + plywood dividers in bundle; share packing photos |

## Section 9: Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) for Divya Stones

### 9.1 Primary ICP — Regional Stone Distributor

**Who they are:**
A family-owned or privately-held regional stone distribution company with **3–10 warehouse locations**, serving a multi-state territory. Annual revenue in the range of **USD 8–50 million**. They import directly from India (or would like to), sell to fabricators, kitchen and bath showrooms, and commercial contractors. They have an established relationship with 1–2 Indian granite suppliers but either do not carry Verde Guatemala or are unhappy with their current green marble supplier's quality/communication.

**Demographics:**
- Owner-operated or 2nd-generation family business
- Located in: Texas (Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston), Florida (Tampa, Miami, Orlando), Georgia (Atlanta), California (Los Angeles, Sacramento), or North Carolina (Charlotte)
- 10–50 employees

**Psychographics:**
- Values long-term relationships with suppliers over lowest-cost procurement
- Frustrated by quality surprises and communication breakdowns with Indian suppliers
- Interested in differentiating their slab yard with unique materials (green marble is not universally stocked)
- Open to visiting India or attending Coverings to meet new suppliers face-to-face
- Responds well to professional sample kits, a clear price list, and a supplier who picks up the phone

**Buying behaviour:**
- Trial order: 1 container (20-ft FCL, ~25 MT, ~1,800–2,000 sq m)
- Payment terms: 30% TT advance, 70% against B/L copy (or LC at sight for first order)
- Reorder cycle: Every 3–4 months if inventory turns
- Average order value: USD 35,000–75,000 per container FOB

**Trigger events (what makes them call a new supplier):**
- Current Verde Guatemala supplier raised prices or cut quality
- They saw another distributor's slab yard carrying green marble and are missing sales
- They attended Coverings or TISE and met an Indian exhibitor
- They received a cold sample kit in the mail and liked what they saw
- A fabricator customer requested green marble they don't carry

### 9.2 Secondary ICP — Hospitality Stone Procurement Specialist

**Who they are:**
A procurement manager or interior design firm that sources stone for **hotel construction, resort renovation, or luxury commercial projects** — lobbies, feature walls, bar tops, bathroom surrounds. They work on project timelines of 12–24 months and require large matched lots (consistent material from a single block or series of adjacent blocks).

**Why they fit:**
Verde Guatemala's dramatic colourway (dark forest green with white calcite veining) is ideal for hospitality accent features. The colour is unique enough to be a design statement but refined enough for luxury environments.

**Key requirement from supplier:**
- Ability to reserve / hold a matched lot for 90–180 days
- Provide bookmatched slab pairs for feature wall applications
- Supply project documentation (ASTM test reports, material data sheets, country of origin certificates)

## Section 10: Entry Strategy Recommendation

### 10.1 Recommended Approach

**Phase 1 — Foundation (Months 1–3):**
- Create a professional, English-language product page for Verde Guatemala on Divya Stones' website with high-resolution slab photography, technical specs, and a sample request form

- Register on **StoneContact.com** as a verified exporter; create a listing specific to Verde Guatemala
- Engage a **US-licensed customs broker** (e.g., Shapiro, CHB International) to prepare a customs classification opinion and tariff estimate — this becomes a sales tool ("we know your landed cost exactly")
- Obtain **ASTM C503 test report** for Verde Guatemala from an NABL-accredited Indian lab

**Phase 2 — Lead Generation (Months 2–5):**
- Build a list of 50 target distributors in TX, FL, GA, CA using ImportGenius or Volza (search HTS 6802 importers)
- Send sample kits to top 20 with a personalised letter explaining:
  - Divya Stones' quarry location (Kesariyaji belt — authentic source)
  - Current tariff situation and estimated landed cost
  - Quality differentiation commitments (pre-shipment inspection, ASTM reports, 24-hr comms)
- Follow up by WhatsApp or email within 1 week of kit delivery

**Phase 3 — Trade Show Presence (Month 4–6 / Next Cycle):**
- Apply to exhibit at **Coverings 2027** (or TISE 2027 StonExpo) through CAPEXIL India pavilion

**Phase 4 — First Order and Beyond (Month 6–12):**
- First trial orders expected from 2–3 distributors at 1 FCL each
- Offer **pre-shipment video inspection** (30-minute WhatsApp video call walking buyer through the specific slabs being packed)
- Provide a **dedicated US-facing contact** (English-speaking, available 9am–6pm EST via WhatsApp)
- Build toward 1 or 2 anchor US customers doing 4–6 containers/year before expanding
